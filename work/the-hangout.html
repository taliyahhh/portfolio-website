Spring 2026 · IDSN-522, Inclusive Design and Design Allyship

The Hangout

Problem statement

A tween girl who feels pressure about how she looks online needs to find a low-pressure place to figure out who she is, but third spaces have disappeared and social media fills the gap.

  • TeamNatalie Guldalian, Sierra McRay, Bradli Powell, Maia Weingard and me

How It Went

  1. StartResearch

    Where It Started

    I came in curious about third spaces, and how few of them are left now that so much of life has moved online. One teammate cared about mental health. Another wanted to focus on teen girls. Put together, that was our niche: third spaces where teen girls can actually thrive.

  2. ResearchResearch

    The In-Between Is Collapsing

    “Tween” started in the 1980s as a marketing term. Now the stage itself is shrinking. Girls 10 to 13 have adult beauty routines, and in 2023 tweens 8 to 12 drove 49% of U.S. skincare sales growth.

    Source: Figures as cited in our toolkit

  3. ResearchResearch

    Third Spaces

    Third spaces are Ray Oldenburg's term for places outside home and school: parks, libraries, diners. Most of them are gone or cost money. When there's nowhere to go, identity gets built online, out of whatever the algorithm surfaces.

  4. SolutionDesign

    The Hangout

    Free pop-ups in libraries, parks and community centers, built around four kinds of activities: creative expression, social connection, physical play and STEAM. Brands can bring materials or expertise, but nothing gets sold.

    • No likes, followers or public metrics
    • Drop in, drop out, nothing graded
    • Walk-ins always welcome, so no phone is required
  5. SolutionProduct

    A Companion App

    The app tracks events, badges and photo booth pictures. There's no messaging. Parents approve every event and get a pickup code at check-in.

  6. My partDesign

    What I Made

    I helped design the toolkit itself, and made the cover, Goals, Third Spaces and Design Guidelines pages.

  7. GuidelinesDesign

    Design Guidelines

    Six inclusive design principles for anyone building a space like this:

    • Recognize exclusion
    • Design for a range of needs
    • Provide flexibility and choice
    • Reduce physical and social barriers
    • Prioritize clarity and simplicity
    • Support independence and belonging

Looking Back

What worked
The idea. I showed it to my tween cousin, who said it was great and that she'd want to share it with her friends or join in. Her friends liked it too.
